Aftercare Registry

Privacy, data and pilot safety

Pilot privacy notice · draft dated 13 September 2026, updated 23 September 2026

Practical help, with careful boundaries.

This notice explains the pilot’s current data practices in plain language. It is a product draft, not a lawyer-approved privacy policy. Obtain Australian privacy and legal review before entering real family information or launching commercially.

During testing, use fictional family and supporter details only. This prototype is not an emergency, counselling, medical, child-safety, transport, booking or payment service.

Public and private information

Helpers see a family display name, general area, welcome message, safe-to-share boundaries and suitable Care Block descriptions. Public-notice links hide trusted-circle Care Blocks. Home addresses, family contacts, case references, dietary details, access notes and helper contact details stay on authorised coordinator screens.

Purpose and consent

Data is used only to create the registry, coordinate Care Blocks through offer, approval and completion, arrange professional or community fallback when nobody from the trusted circle is available, manage dates and evaluate the pilot. A coordinator must record family consent and agreement to the limited use of helper contact details before activating a registry.

Information collected

The pilot may hold family identification and contact details, practical preferences, Care Blocks, helper name and phone, optional helper email, proposed and confirmed dates, status history, and — when a Care Block is arranged through fallback — the provider’s name and coordinator notes. It does not collect card details, take payments, or send automatic SMS.

Retention, export and deletion

Each registry has a standard 90-day support period and a review date 30 days after it ends, both adjustable by the coordinator. Records are not automatically deleted in the pilot. An authorised coordinator can mark a registry complete, archive it, download its record, replace helper links and permanently delete the registry and related data. Downloaded copies are then the recipient’s responsibility.

Access and account separation

Coordinator areas require the pilot PIN. In this version all registries sit in one shared workspace; separate organisations, a distinct family/recipient login, and permission levels (owner, administrator, coordinator, view-only) are not part of it. Helper links and commitment links are unlisted bearer links: anyone who receives one may be able to use it, so they should be shared carefully and replaced if disclosed unintentionally.

Service providers and future integrations

The Site’s hosting and database infrastructure (Cloudflare Pages and D1) process the information needed to operate the pilot. Page fonts (Fraunces and Inter) are loaded from Google Fonts. SMS, email, payment and professional booking providers are not connected. Before any provider is added, its terms, data location, security, consent wording and deletion process should be reviewed and this notice updated.

Correction, questions or an incident

Ask the coordinator who shared the registry to correct, export, close or delete a record. If a link or contact detail is exposed, close the registry, replace its supporter links and notify the pilot owner promptly. A commercial launch needs a named privacy contact and documented incident-response process.

Safety

Do not put health information, access codes or children’s identifying details in public descriptions. If something feels unsafe, do not proceed and contact the coordinator. For immediate danger in Australia, call 000.
